Build notes

  • Echo divider: the JSN-SR04T drives Echo at 5 V. With 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ, GPIO32 sees about 3.3 V. Don't skip it, because the ESP32 inputs aren't 5 V tolerant.
  • Supply divider: 100 kΩ + 100 kΩ halves the 5 V rail to about 2.5 V on GPIO34, which matches VBAT_DIVIDER_RATIO = 2.0, so no firmware change is needed. batt_mv will read about 4700–5000 mV. If it drops below about 4500 mV, the supply or the cable is sagging.
  • Skip the divider? It's optional. If you leave it out, tie GPIO34 to GND with a 100 kΩ resistor so the pin doesn't float, and ignore batt_mv.
  • Long USB runs: thin USB cables over a few metres can drop below what the JSN-SR04T needs. Mount the plug pack near the tank, or run 5 V over thicker figure-8 cable and keep only the last metre as USB.
  • DS18B20 colours: red/yellow/black is the usual scheme, but some probes use white or blue for data. Check the probe's listing before soldering.
  • JSN-SR04T mode: leave the board in its default trigger/echo mode (no mode resistor fitted). It can't measure closer than about 20–25 cm.

Parts for one node

  • Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V2, 915 MHz version, with antenna
  • JSN-SR04T waterproof ultrasonic module
  • DS18B20 waterproof probe
  • SSD1306 0.96" I²C OLED (optional once deployed)
  • Resistors: 1 kΩ, 2 kΩ, 4.7 kΩ, 2 × 100 kΩ
  • 100 nF ceramic capacitor
  • 5 V 2 A USB plug pack and micro-USB cable (outdoor-rated power point or a weatherproof box at the tank)
Heltec V2 pin clash: on a genuine V2, GPIO4 is also the onboard OLED's SDA line and GPIO21 switches power to the external pins (Vext). If the DS18B20 reads 85 °C, reads −127 °C or drops out, move DQ to GPIO13 and change PIN_DS18B20 to 13. Also, never transmit with the antenna disconnected, because it can damage the SX1276.
